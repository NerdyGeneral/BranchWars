This document consolidates the Branch Wars Codex Implementation Blueprint v6 PDF and four subsequent financial-empire and national-scale proposals. It is the new planning baseline. Preserve v6 and the source proposals as design history; do not overwrite them.

The intended game is a long-form, simultaneous-turn financial strategy game. Two friends begin as competing banks in a populated national economy and can develop regional networks, products, financial-services subsidiaries, and corporate holdings. Banking is the starting business, not the limit of the campaign.

This blueprint authorizes no code changes by itself. Implement a selected, bounded work package only when requested. Claims in source documents about code versions, test results, defects, or completed upgrades are historical evidence; inspect the current repository before relying on them.

Requirement precedence: explicit current user decisions; this blueprint's decision register and invariants; the selected work package's acceptance criteria; compatibility constraints and verified current behavior; v6 for non-conflicting background. Tests are evidence, not authority to preserve a superseded design bug.

## 2. Product pillars

1. **Power creates exposure.** Growth requires funding, service capacity, management, and capital. Diversification can reduce one risk while increasing operational complexity.
2. **The rival is present.** Players contest customers, contracts, talent, channels, investment opportunities, and acquisition control.
3. **The country changes the strategy.** Sector mix, wealth, employment, rates, confidence, and infrastructure make regions different.
4. **Consequences persist.** Contracts, loan vintages, customer loyalty, staffing, technology debt, and acquisitions carry effects into later turns.
5. **Results are explainable.** A player can trace material changes to decisions, rival pressure, economic conditions, and realized events.
6. **Scale changes management.** Local optimization gives way to regional budgets and group capital allocation without making local businesses irrelevant.
7. **Depth is not compulsory busywork.** Policies persist; managers handle routine work; players intervene in consequential exceptions.

Before accepting a mechanic, specify what it enables, its cost and exposure, rival counterplay, a reasonable alternative, and how its result is explained. Reject disconnected reward buttons.

## 3. Campaign contract and pacing

### 3.1 Modes

**National Rivalry:** two primary competitors plus supporting institutions. Setup exposes the victory objective and institutional-failure rules. Victory requires a sustained, announced objective or a deliberate, financed takeover; franchise value informs decisions but does not silently resolve the game. No hard turn limit.

**Empire:** milestones do not end the campaign. Players may continue developing their groups. Insolvency remains consequential; this is not immunity from failure. A failed subsidiary may be resolved while a viable parent survives. Human parent-level failure uses the explicitly selected campaign rule.

**Local introduction:** a smaller scenario with fewer systems initially exposed, using the same authoritative accounting and competition rules.

Open before campaign implementation: exact Rivalry objective thresholds; human parent-failure ending versus optional successor institution. Do not silently grant a failed player a free replacement bank. Dual failure has an explicit outcome, not a fabricated healthy acquirer.

### 3.2 Provisional scale and duration

- Initial vertical slice: two regions, approximately six markets, two humans or a human and an AI, and a small supporting-institution roster.
- National content target: 8-12 regions and 40-60 economically distinct markets.
- National pacing target: 120-200 meaningful monthly turns, not a minimum enforced duration.
- Typical simultaneous planning target: 3-6 minutes per turn; approximately 6-20 hours for the proposed campaign range before additional breaks/reviews.
- Save and resume across many sessions is a core requirement.

Validate these targets through human play. Do not extend project timers merely to reach a turn count. Monthly costs and probabilities use explicit period/annualization helpers; any future time conversion is a separately tested migration.

### 3.3 Development stages

Local establishment -> regional expansion -> national competition -> financial conglomerate.

Stages are descriptive, overlapping state thresholds, not scheduled turn gates. They unlock opportunities through infrastructure, expertise, scale, and financing. They do not remove re-entry rights or automatically activate forced consolidation.

### 3.4 Permanent optional complexity

**Required design rule:** meaningful optional systems remain campaign-configurable even when mature. A **Preview** label describes implementation maturity and acceptance limits, not a temporary exemption before compulsory integration. Removing that label must not silently remove the player's complexity choice. Not every internal detail needs its own switch: related mechanics may form a coherent, dependency-valid module.

Authoritative accounting, deterministic resolution, validation, save integrity, privacy/security boundaries and multiplayer synchronization are core contracts, not optional difficulty settings. Optional modules use those same contracts. Complexity controls how many interacting systems the players manage; difficulty controls the challenge. Neither permits hidden AI bonuses or rule exemptions.

Show prerequisites and the effective selected rules before starting. Reject invalid combinations or require an explicit, visible prerequisite change; never silently run a partial dependency chain. The current v8.13 pilot's preview prerequisites are largely cumulative, not independently composable switches. That compatibility chain is not the permanent modular-complexity target: retain minimal genuine dependencies and mandatory foundations, then allow optional systems to compose in tested supported profiles rather than requiring every earlier preview forever.

Proposed **Streamlined**, **Deeper** and **Custom** profiles should make valid choices understandable without implying that deeper always means harder. They may select only delivered, supported modules, not enable unavailable group or national content. Presets remain deferred; the current modularity batch delivers the shared feature catalog and dependency-confirmed selector, subject to its release gates.

In multiplayer, the host selects one shared campaign ruleset. Both players review the same effective rules; pre-game rule changes reset readiness and require compatible peer versions before starting. The current modularity batch implements host drafts, a committed rules/dependency summary and connection-bound compatibility; preset controls remain deferred. Existing transport/version safeguards remain mandatory.

Persist campaign-start flags in saves and carry them through resume, reconnect and rematch unless a new campaign explicitly selects different rules. Rules are generally fixed once a campaign starts: disabling a system must not erase customer accounts, pending work, contracts, funding obligations or losses. Any future mid-campaign rule change needs its own explicit migration and obligation-preserving acceptance tests. Existing saves retain their established rules and behavior; maturity or a new default never silently upgrades them.

## 4. Living nation and persistent competition

### 4.1 Geographic hierarchy

**Nation:** corporate policies, group financing, subsidiaries, national brands, acquisitions, systemic conditions.

**Region:** leadership, hubs, budgets, industry exposure, management capacity, regional competitors.

**Market:** customer segments, facilities, relationships, local prices and service, competitive activity.

Markets carry stable IDs, adjacency, regional membership, population/business opportunity, sector composition, wealth, growth, saturation, confidence, and infrastructure. National totals aggregate from modeled local positions; a market is not a decorative score card.

Physical adjacency and hubs reduce expansion and management burdens. Disconnected entry remains possible at a defined cost. Digital reach is a different distribution model with acquisition, trust, support, and infrastructure costs, not free nationwide presence.

### 4.2 Market leadership and withdrawal

Track leadership by segment and business line rather than one exclusive owner. Retail deposits, commercial relationships, lending, insurance, and wealth can have different leaders.

Low share may cause a facility to become uneconomic, trigger retreat pressure, or justify closure. It never permanently bars the institution from the market in national modes. Re-entry routes include a physical office, digital product, anchor contract, distribution partnership, or acquisition.

Incumbents benefit from relationships and operating infrastructure. Challengers can exploit poor service, expiring contracts, unmet needs, or a better product. Re-entry has genuine cost and ramp-up; avoid free repeated entry/exit exploits.

No unexplained franchise dividends or free branches from territorial capture. Acquired earnings require an operating business, integration, and ongoing expenses.

### 4.3 Supporting institutions

Use simplified but explicit regional banks, credit unions, digital firms, insurers, and brokerages. They have bounded customers, business mix, finances, offers, and strategic responses. They cannot create assets or customers without modeled sources.

Supporting institutions provide competition, partnership choices, acquisition targets, and contested bids. They may be aggregated where detail provides no player value. Their simplification must not create privileged trading or impossible acquisitions.

Three or more human seats remain later scope: remove two-player assumptions across targeting, ownership, privacy, resolution, and transports together, not only in setup UI.

## 5. Banking and customer economics

### 5.1 Accounting contract

- Maintain reconcilable entity balance sheets: assets equal liabilities plus equity within defined rounding tolerance.
- Deposit inflow increases bank cash and its deposit liability; ordinary withdrawal reduces both. It is not revenue or an automatic equity loss.
- Lending, repayment, external funding, fees, expenses, securities transactions, credit losses, and distributions each have explicit postings and counterparties/boundaries.
- Capital adequacy uses designed risk-bearing exposures, not deposit liabilities as risk assets.
- Liquidity stress follows visible cash, available normal funding/liquid assets, emergency funding or asset disposal, and explicit realized-loss consequences.
- Distinguish cash movement, accrual income, asset revaluation, and realized loss. Accounting treatment is a documented game abstraction, not a claim of real-world regulatory completeness.
- Customer assets under custody/management are not spendable group assets. Intercompany flows eliminate on consolidation; consolidated profit cannot double-count subsidiary transfers.
- Negative equity is a possible failure state, not something to clamp away. Nonnegative constraints apply only where semantically valid.

### 5.2 Customer and portfolio model

Use aggregated cohorts for scale, with named anchor relationships for memorable competition. Customers may hold multiple products; do not count each account as a new unique person. Define each share denominator and conservation boundary.

Local balances move among players, supporting institutions, and explicit economic inflow/outflow. Finite opportunity does not mean a permanently fixed economy: growth, contraction, credit creation, and external flows must be modeled and attributable.

Funding includes checking, savings/high-yield, term deposits, business operating balances, and wholesale/emergency sources. Each has pricing, sensitivity, stickiness, maturity, and concentration characteristics.

Loans include consumer, mortgage, small business, commercial/industrial, and commercial property. Record yield, underwriting quality, vintage, duration, delinquency, loss, and sector/geographic exposure. Aggressive origination affects future losses, not only an immediate risk meter.

### 5.3 Product lifecycle

Products carry target segment, region/channel availability, rates, fees, service promise, eligibility, staffing/technology dependencies, acquisition cost, and ongoing economics.

Lifecycle: develop -> deploy -> launch -> grow -> maintain/reprice -> retire. Multiple products operate simultaneously. Contract terms persist according to product rules; repricing an offer does not rewrite locked existing contracts. Retirement services existing obligations and stops or redirects new acquisition.

Show product contribution, funding usage, capacity demand, retention, losses, and concentration. Distinguish direct contribution from allocated overhead so internal transfer pricing does not invent profit.

## 6. Facilities, departments, and management

### 6.1 Facilities

Facility types include micro/ATM service, retail branch, commercial office, wealth office, financial center, and regional hub. Each has cost, upkeep, staffing, throughput, ramp-up, local demand, condition, and hub links.

Upgrade paths include retail service, commercial expertise, wealth advisory, automation, and resilience. Support conversion, consolidation, renovation, and closure with customer disruption and costs. No universal upgrade should improve every business without a trade-off.

Facility screens show attributed customers, deposits, lending, service utilization, expenses, and contribution. Attribution rules must avoid counting the same income at both branch and regional levels without reconciliation.

### 6.2 Departments and capacity

Departments cover sales/relationships, onboarding/service, lending/credit administration, servicing/collections, operations, technology, risk/compliance, treasury, and people management. Subsidiaries add specialist functions when introduced.

Each has budget, staff/skills, leader, workload, service level, training, and outsourcing options. Growth creates demand before service failures arise. Hiring takes time to become productive; outsourcing trades flexibility for cost, dependency, and control.

Executive initiative capacity governs major change. Routine policies do not consume action tokens. Distinguish project-delivery capacity from operational capacity.

Named leaders influence specific functions and carry compensation, loyalty, workload, succession, and performance histories. Avoid universally superior executive bonuses.

### 6.3 Delegation

Regional managers follow persistent mandates and spending/risk limits. Support templates, bulk changes, budget caps, reserve requirements, stop conditions, and previews. Automation cannot secretly override the player's locked plan or exceed delegated authority. Material automated actions appear in the ledger.

## 7. Research, deployment, and institutional identity

Keep open capability investment, with recurring budgets and editable priorities. Stop funding at completion or a player-selected milestone; do not silently divert excess spending.

Separate discovery/development, deployment to a business or region, and ongoing operation/training. Research unlocks behavior and operating models, not only percentage inflation.

Example applications:

| Capability combination | Application | Continuing burden |
|---|---|---|
| Digital + credit/data | Automated underwriting | Data quality, monitoring, exception handling |
| Commercial + technology | Corporate treasury services | Specialist support, reliability |
| Network + operations | Regional service hubs | Staffing and regional overhead |
| Acquisition + operations | Integration teams | Delivery capacity and retention costs |
| Digital + brokerage infrastructure | Investment accounts | Custody/platform support and controls |
| Distribution + insurance expertise | Insurance agency | Producer training and partner management |

Allow build, buy, and partner routes where economically meaningful. Deep specialization should create opportunity costs and operational commitments, not an arbitrary permanent ban on diversification. Institutional identity describes actual choices and vulnerabilities.

## 8. Marketing and renewable relationships

Advertising specifies product, audience, geography, channel, budget, and duration. Track awareness -> applications -> activation -> retained relationships -> contribution. Report acquisition cost and retention after incentives expire, not only gross deposits attracted.

Reach has saturation and diminishing returns. Marketing interacts with product competitiveness, service capacity, trust, and rival offers. Demand that onboarding cannot serve creates waste and possible reputational consequences.

Named companies have sector, employees, finances, funding needs, and renewable banking/service contracts. Payroll, treasury, credit, insurance, and retirement services can be won separately. Contract terms, switching costs, renewal windows, and service failures create continuing competition.

Example: a manufacturer awards payroll to one player but borrows from the other. Its expansion creates cross-selling opportunities; a downturn can damage both its loans and a player's equity stake. Winning one relationship does not automatically award all products.

## 9. Financial-services group and ownership

### 9.1 Subsidiaries

A holding company allocates capital among separately modeled businesses. Each subsidiary has earnings, expenses, staffing/capacity, commitments, risk, and distribution arrangements. Funding transfers and shared services have explicit rules. A viable parent is not automatically destroyed by every subsidiary failure, nor may it freely raid protected customer assets.

Delivery order:

1. Insurance agency: partner products and commission income, distribution/service obligations.
2. Brokerage: self-service accounts, platform and custody costs, transaction/service income.
3. Wealth management: adviser teams, assets under management, recurring fees, suitability/service capacity, market-driven client behavior.
4. Insurance underwriting later: premiums, reserves, claim development, exposure accumulation, reinsurance, and catastrophe stress.

Agency and underwriting are distinct strategies, not successive free upgrades. Brokerage assets under custody and wealth assets under management are memorandum/customer metrics, not bank deposits or spendable equity.

### 9.2 Company shares and transactions

Introduce a bounded fictional company roster. Track issued shares, outside float, holdings, valuation drivers, dividends, and liquidity. Minority ownership does not grant automatic service contracts or control.

Define simultaneous order clearing, cash reservation, price impact, partial fills, fees, and ownership conservation before exposing trading controls. Prohibit unlimited trades against stale prices. Dividends require issuer resources and do not create money from nothing.

Control acquisitions use interest -> valuation/diligence -> financing -> offer/control contest -> review -> integration -> realization. Include competing bids and defensive choices. Acquisition proceeds and acquired liabilities reconcile; integration can harm an overextended buyer.

Initially exclude circular ownership, leverage-based trading, and elaborate derivatives. Rival-bank share accumulation comes after basic company ownership and takeover rules are verified.

## 10. Economy, intelligence, crisis, and counterplay

Economy state includes rates, employment, growth, inflation, confidence, housing/property, sector conditions, and systemic stress. Regions carry different exposures. Events follow signal -> pressure -> trigger -> impact -> secondary effects -> aftermath where appropriate.

Competitive contests target funding, relationships, talent, distribution, territory, or transaction control. Effects scale to available local pools and commitments. Local defenses have local costs; enterprise programs are separately defined. Heat represents observable fatigue, skepticism, scrutiny, or escalation rather than a universal arbitrary penalty.

Information classes: public, estimated, legitimately acquired intelligence, hidden. Estimates include confidence and freshness. Previews show ranges and known drivers, never exact outcomes derived from sealed rival plans.

Regulation uses visible stages, findings, restrictions, deadlines, and remediation. Retain v6's progression from monitoring through formal restrictions and resolution, but tune complexity for usability. Crisis UI shows liquidity runway, causal losses, deadlines, recovery options, and costs. Distress is playable before terminal failure.

Systemic shocks alter opportunities and funding conditions; they do not automatically end the game. Temporary cooperation, asset purchases, and predatory competition can coexist. No hidden AI bonuses.

## 11. UI and turn experience

Use a contemporary financial strategy interface, not a retro terminal or military command fantasy. Geographic views may use isometric local scenes where helpful; buildings must communicate actual facilities and economics. Brightness/theme polish is secondary to hierarchy and readability.

Primary workspaces:

- **Command Center:** turn briefing, ranked decisions, group health, watch list.
- **Markets:** national -> region -> local navigation with a persistent detail drawer and segment overlays.
- **Group:** bank balance sheet, business-line earnings, subsidiaries, products, facilities, departments.
- **Competition:** active contests, rival activity, contracts, offers, defenses.
- **Strategy:** capability web, deployments, capital allocation, project timelines, talent, acquisitions.
- **Intelligence:** regional economy, estimates, stress scenarios, histories and comparisons.

Keep command metrics, phase/sync status, and planning commitments available without a page-long scroll. Use progressive disclosure and bounded detail views, not duplicate card grids or mandatory modal stacks.

Every material metric opens a concise cause breakdown. Board Report bridges reconcile deposits, earnings, credit, cash/liquidity, capital, market positions, staffing, projects, and transactions. Separate operating forecasts from full-turn cash projections and realized results.

Each decision shows upfront and recurring cost, capacity, principal downside, dependencies, expected range, and uncertainty. Budget previews use the same authoritative cost functions as validation.

Ready, locked, resolving, reviewing, and reconnecting are distinct states. Reversible ready is not the same as a sealed commitment. Respect keyboard access, readable contrast, reduced motion, responsive layouts, and number formatting that does not hide material changes.

## 12. Technical and multiplayer contract

Retain a single-file playable release if useful, but introduce tested seams for state, accounting, resolution, content, AI, views, and transports. Do not combine wholesale modularization with economic changes.

Canonical records: campaign/version/RNG; economy; regions/markets; institutions/group/entities; cohorts/contracts/portfolios; facilities/departments/talent; products/capabilities/deployments; marketing/contests; companies/shares/orders; regulatory matters; structured event ledger.

Each material event has ID, turn, category, actor/target, accounting or quantity changes, visibility, and parent cause. Human-readable reports derive from structured events. Compact older history through reconciled checkpoints without losing save validity.

Define and test a stable resolution order: validate/reserve commitments; resolve sealed simultaneous contests/orders under explicit tie rules; apply due contracts/operations/projects according to documented activation timing; settle liquidity/losses; assess regulation/end conditions; publish redacted reports. A work package must specify exact timing of its new effects, not rely on incidental function order.

Use deterministic authoritative RNG, stable iteration/tie behavior, schema and engine versions, and repeatable replay. Previewing cannot consume RNG or mutate state. Same inputs and version yield the same state in every transport.

All local/hotseat/LAN/P2P/Repository Link modes share rules, validation, and migration. Transports carry versioned intents/revisions with idempotency and explicit reconnect reconciliation. No credential enters saves, reports, replay, or game state. Treat transport privacy and host access honestly: UI redaction alone does not protect secrets from an authoritative host or a repository reader who can access full state.

Open technical gate: document the trusted-friends threat model and where sealed plans reside. Do not claim adversarial cheat resistance without an architecture that supports it.

Preserve old saves through explicit migration or a clearly separated legacy engine path. Never reinterpret old counters, holdings, or contracts silently. Test exports, rematches, reconnects, interrupted writes, duplicate submissions, and mixed-version rejection.

## 13. Validation and fun gates

### 13.1 Correctness

Test accounting and quantity reconciliation; valid local targeting; portfolio maturity; share ownership and trading settlement; subsidiary consolidation; public-state privacy; deterministic replay; simultaneous terminal conditions; capacity/budget boundaries; migration and transports.

For every new mechanic add positive, boundary, and failure cases. Test two full turns around activation/maturity boundaries, not just button availability. Run current engine, transport, balance, and Windows LAN suites as applicable; an automated LAN test does not replace two-computer acceptance.

For configurable complexity, publish a bounded set of supported dependency profiles and test their creation, save/resume, rematch, previews and transport compatibility. Cover each optional module's off/on boundary with its prerequisites, representative supported interactions, invalid dependency rejection, readiness reset and unsupported-peer refusal. Do not promise exhaustive coverage of all 2^N combinations or imply that an untested combination is supported.

### 13.2 Strategic balance

Measure paired-seat packages and intended AI strategies, not only the identity label observed at campaign end. Include scenario/region diversity, action usage and counters, investment returns, concentration, recovery, acquisition outcomes, and late-game scale.

Retain v6's provisional investigation bands: overall comparable packages around 46-54%, seat share around 48-52%, investigate sustained results outside bands. Do not force every scenario or legitimate asymmetric strategy to exactly 50%.

Use at least 5,000 paired-seat seeded campaigns for a major balance release when feasible, with confidence and sample sizes reported. Separate ending modes: a completion target applies to Rivalry audits; open-ended Empire runs test stable progression and meaningful decisions, not forced termination. ### 13.3 Human-playtest gates

- At least three distinct strategic approaches prove viable in human matches.
- Record planning time, repetitive clicks, neglected controls, and turns with no meaningful choice.

Prototype acceptance includes observed human evidence; simulated win rates alone cannot pass the fun gate.

### 18.5 M2 — Price-sensitive rivalry, using the existing contest

Player alternatives: fund a more competitive tariff, defend through current
service/branch investments, keep margin, or shift existing sales targets. No
mandatory price matching. A rich incumbent pays the higher rate on its existing
variable book too; a small challenger receives no special bonus.

change service goodwill/churn rates, or treat price as an instant reputation gain.

Concrete proposed price-edge contract:
1. After both banks operate and delever, immediately before the existing contest,
   build one immutable snapshot for all markets. Do not let an earlier market's
   transfers change a later market's price weights.
2. For each market, consider both banks' nonlocked, remaining=0 cohorts. A cohort
   is price-addressable only where both banks can currently market its product
   to its segment (developed, non-retired and positive existing sales target).
   Guarantees and locked accounts never earn new price pressure. The baseline
   contest's existing handling of withdrawable guarantees is otherwise unchanged.
3. Weight addressable cohorts by their principal; divide by the combined
   withdrawable principal in that market, including still-guaranteed withdrawable
   accounts. Empty denominator means zero price contribution. Thus a small
   eligible pool cannot provide a full-book pricing bonus.
4. For each bank, convert its effective rounded tariff add-on for those cohorts
   back to annual bp. Positive add-ons are multiplied by clamp(current existing
   householdServiceReview coverage, 0, 1); negative add-ons are not excused by poor
   service. No extra staff cost, service pool or goodwill increment is created.
5. Provisional D06 signal per bank = 0.75 * weighted effective bp / 25, clipped to
   [-0.75, +0.75]. Add signal0 - signal1 to the normal edge (maximum +/-1.5).
   Apply only to player-versus-player comparison; outside defense keeps its
   existing pull calculation. The contribution is zero with all add-ons neutral.
6. Settle the original single local transfer, cohort preservation, customer
   movement and funding consequence. Do not count competitive transfers as new
   onboarding applications or charge outside-acquisition fees for them.

deposit contest**, not a universal consumer utility model. Ordinary outside intake
continues to respond to the existing global policy, product fit and advertising;

its guarantee and therefore has no editable tariff or new price pressure in this
slice. Its existing fit, cost and guarantee tradeoffs remain. A later expansion
